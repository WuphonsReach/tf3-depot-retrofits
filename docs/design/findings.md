# Findings

## In short

- **Where the values come from.** A depot's maintenance pool, radius, noise, pollution and upkeep are all set by the construction's update script when the building is built. No game command changes them afterwards, so the mod wraps each depot construction's update script, reads level parameters, and rebuilds the construction to apply a level.
- **The method works, tested in game.** A rebuild keeps the construction and depot entity ids, keeps every vehicle's line, costs nothing, and applies all five values: pool, radius, noise, pollution (the emitter power follows the scaled values exactly) and upkeep. A game script can charge the level's price with a journal entry.
- **The cost of a rebuild: vehicles are reshuffled.** Every rebuild, even one that changes nothing, clears the maintenance link of every vehicle the building maintained. Within a game day the game assigns each vehicle again, by its own choice: in the test the maintenance buildings got all their vehicles back, the depot none of its 10 (they went to a nearer maintenance building and stayed there for the whole year). No command sets a vehicle's maintenance station, so the mod cannot put them back. Over the following months, the larger pool and radius draw vehicles in: the rebuilt maintenance building grew from 9 to 13 vehicles, the rebuilt depot later took 6 from a neighbour.
- **Pollution and noise.** The base game cannot green depots: the Industry Greenification tool only reaches industries. Depot emissions come from the same construction data, so the rebuild covers both trees. A larger pool also lowers emissions by itself, since the emission state follows vehicles in maintenance against the pool.
- **The pollution map hardly shows a level.** The map and its cursor number show pollution in decibels, averaged over time: 12 steps of about 1.9 dB from 57 to 80 dB, with 12 meaning 80 dB or more. Cutting a building's pollution by 45% is −2.6 dB, about 1.4 steps where the building is the only source; a full tree (−75%) is −6 dB, about 3 steps. Passing vehicles are their own sources and keep their pollution. Measured in game on a rail maintenance building alone in a field: the live value at the building fell exactly by 10 log10 of the power (50 → 27.5 at level 9: −2.60 dB), and the averaged value the map shows follows it with a time constant of about 190,000 game-clock units, about 3 minutes of play at 1x speed (48 game days at calendar speed 1x). So the map gets two-thirds of the way in about 3 minutes and settles in about 10; on that building it went from 11 to 10.
- **The town's rating graph shows a level best.** The Pollution Rating graph at the bottom of the town window's pollution card plots the town's whole pollution as a rating, where 1 point is 0.14 dB, and hovering a point shows its value. Pollution 9 on three of Wigton's buildings (a ship depot, a road depot and a road maintenance building) raised it from about 80% to 87–89% within a few minutes at 4x, against a wobble of about ±2 points: −1.0 to −1.3 dB, a fifth to a quarter less pollution over the town. In the larger Dereham (about 1,150 residents), pollution 11 on all 12 of its depots and maintenance buildings, which gave off a third of its pollution, took the rating from a flat ~41% to 45% for 1974 (the year of the buys), 49% for 1975 and 50% for 1976, then 49% for 1977 and 47% for 1978 as its industries and vehicles grew; it stayed Mediocre throughout. The shares predict about +6 points (−0.9 dB); stations and tracks, about half of the town's pollution before the buys, are out of the mod's reach. The source list above the graph cannot show this: each row is a share of the town's total, and industries change their pollution in steps as they start and stop producing, so every other row jumps with them (Wigton's depots read 14%, 23% or 33% within a minute, with no change to the depots).
- **Saved with the game.** Loading a save does not rerun the construction scripts: the rebuilt buildings came back with their scaled values and level params, and the wrapper was not called. So a change to the level formulas reaches a levelled building only at its next rebuild. Until then it keeps the upkeep the old formula gave, and the tab, which works the build price out back from that upkeep with the new formula, shows its prices and upkeep figures wrong (25% to 43% too high after the upkeep step went from 25% to 15%, the eleventh load below; 47% on a tram depot with pollution and noise levels bought before those trees got cheaper, the last test below). A buy corrects the building, since it rebuilds it, but that buy is charged the wrong price. The mod does not correct this: it would matter only if a released version changed the upkeep formulas.
- **Keeping levels out of the build menu.** A level param declared with an availability year far in the future (`yearFrom` 3000) does not show in the build menu and is not passed when a depot is placed, yet a rebuild stores it and passes it to the update script. A param that is not declared at all behaves the same way. A declared param with a single value still shows in the menu. The mod declares 9999, since a long game can pass 3000; at 9999 the build menu showed no level options either, and a building placed by hand started at level 0 (probe 0.4).
- **Levels at scale (probe 0.4).** 18 of the save's 24 buildings were given levels by rebuilding them (all five trees, levels 0 to 15); pool, radius, upkeep and stored levels matched the formulas for all 18 at every check up to a game month, and the emitter power followed the scaled emission states.
- **The Retrofit tab and buying work (probe 0.4).** Each wrapped construction gets its own window (`entityWindowScript`) with the depot's tab and a Retrofit tab beside it, like the base Cleaning Plant. It showed on road, rail and ship depots and on road maintenance buildings, including one placed during the run, with the right prices, the right values after the next level, and a greyed "Rank N" button above the claimed rank. A greyed button shows no tooltip when hovered (the game shows none on a disabled button), so its label is all the player sees: "Rank N", or the price alone when money is short (the last test below). Ten buys on two buildings each charged the shown price, rebuilt the building with the new level and matched the formulas. A card added to the base maintenance station window (a `react-plugin` resource) also worked; the mod does not use it.
- **Reading the rank.** The base `company_progression_util.tl` gives the claimed rank (`level`) and the earned rank (`potentialLevel`); the company window reads it the same way. It works from a game script (read rank 2, Mechanic, as the company window showed).
- **Two upkeep figures.** The depot list and `calcMaintenanceForSubconstruction` give the building's yearly upkeep ($37,500 for a road depot). The upkeep footer of the building window adds every entry of the construction's `MAINTENANCE_COST` `accumulatedCost` to it (`MaintenanceBar` in `gui/entity_window/entity_window_util.tl`), which is why it read $38,067 for that depot. A rebuild resets the accumulated costs to 0, and they also fall without one (a rail maintenance building read 11,650 and then 1,218 a few seconds later, as its one vehicle left). No script in the base game writes them: the engine fills them, keyed by pairs of carriers (`MaintenanceCost` in `api/tealdef/api/engine.d.tl`), and they look like the cost of servicing the vehicles. The mod scales only `maintenanceCost`, so the upkeep tree does not cut them. The footer's own upkeep is the construction's `MAINTENANCE_COST` `maintenanceCost` (the update script's value) times the base config's `infrastructureMaintenanceScale` (and the year's cost multiplier for constructions whose costs follow the years), rounded; `calcMaintenanceForSubconstruction` drops the half dollar instead (101,562 where the footer shows 101,563). Prices use `calcMaintenanceForSubconstruction` (the build price is rounded to $100, so the half dollar does not matter); the Retrofit tab's upkeep figures are worked out the footer's way and add the accumulated costs, so they match the footer. The tab works them out from the whole-dollar upkeep before the levels, as the wrapper does: scaling the stored upkeep by the ratio of the factors carried its rounding along and was off by $1 before the scale in 4 of 66 logged buys. Below a factor of 1 several whole-dollar upkeeps round to the same stored one, and the tab takes the roundest (every base depot's and Roundhouse module's upkeep is a multiple of $50).
- **Modular depots.** The Roundhouse mod's depot is built from modules: its update script makes the base (1 vehicle, $60,000 upkeep), and each stall adds a vehicle (in the module's own update script, which the game runs after the construction's) and $100 upkeep (the module's `metadata.maintenanceCost`, which the game adds). The mod counts the modules' upkeep in the construction wrapper and wraps the update scripts of the modules under `depots/` with a `loadModule` modifier, which TF3 calls; without that the levels would scale only the base (the eighth load below). Tested in game: the pool is the formula's pool for the base plus every stall, and the tab's upkeep figures match the footer. Editing a levelled one keeps up: each edit rebuilds it through the wrapper, which recounts the modules' upkeep, so the upkeep stays (base + current modules) × the level factor and the tab's build price and next prices follow the edit (the last test below). Levels bought before an edit are not repriced: buying levels on a small Roundhouse and adding stalls afterwards skips the larger building's one-off price, though the yearly upkeep the levels add grows with every stall. This is accepted.
- **Rebuilds the game starts.** Electrifying a rail depot or a Roundhouse, converting a tram depot's tracks to catenary and adding or removing modules on a levelled Roundhouse all rebuilt the building through the wrapper and kept its levels. These tools go through the depot's upgrade script, which changes only its own keys (`catenary`, `streetTemplate`, `tramCatenary`) and returns the other params as they were (`depots/rail/rail_depot.script.lua`, `depots/road/tram_depot/tram_depot.script.lua`). No base depot or maintenance building has a param that can be changed after building (no `postConstructionModifiable` in their `.con` files).
- **Charging in a run of quick buys.** Of 144 buys made with quick clicks at the fastest speed, 142 were charged the shown price once, and 59 repeated clicks were refused as stale with nothing charged. Two buys landed but went uncharged: the pending buy they were checked against held the levels from before the buy and a deadline already past, which the script never writes. The script's state is written both from the game's simulation thread (the buy queue) and from worker threads (everything else), each with its own copy, which may explain it; the log cannot show it. A pending buy now keeps only its tree and level and counts as landed once the stored level in that tree is at least that high, so such a record still gets charged (the twelfth load below).
- **Buildings that list no depot.** A construction's `depots` list can be empty: all 13 maintenance buildings opened in the mod.io save "333" (road, rail and water, built 1904 to 1998) list none, while its depots of the same years, and a maintenance building placed on the current build, list theirs. That save's header says it was first written by a pre-release build (format 555), the likely cause; which build placed each building is unknown. The base building window does not use that list; it goes through the construction's subconstructions and their `VEHICLE_DEPOT` component (`gui/entity_window/make_entity_window.tl`). The mod falls back to the same, in the tab and in the game script; with it all 13 show the Retrofit tab, and 33 buys on two of them were charged and matched the formulas (the last test below).
- **Where the charges show.** A level's price, booked as depot construction, lands in the Finances tab's Investments row, sub-row Infrastructure, to the dollar. That row's sub-rows (Roads, Tracks, Infrastructure, Vehicles) have no carrier, and the carrier's own row (Water, Rail) does not change, so the carrier the charge is booked under does not show in that table (the last test below).
- **The game can refuse a rebuild.** A rebuild is a construction proposal like any other, and the game checks it: in the "333" save it refused two buildings' rebuilds with `ProposalData error: Collision` (Berkeley Road Depot, 88894, in a dense city block, and a tram building, 217856), every time. What collides is something built around the building since: at the tram building a town house and garden on the lot next door, at Berkeley Road Depot the road at its exit, a crossing (entity 80088), which stayed in the way after the whole block around the depot had been cleared, and a road built again from the depot's exit to that crossing, at either of two angles tried, blocked it again ("Construction Not Possible", no entity named). Levels bought while the depot's road is cut off stay when the road is built again (the last three tests below). The level then never lands, and the game script gives up after the pending deadline with `notLanded` and charges nothing; clicks in between are refused as `pending`. The base building windows and Industry Upgrades send their rebuilds with `ignoreErrors` off; the base game's own in-place replacements (landmark level-ups, mission building swaps) send with it on. What a forced rebuild does on a collision is unknown (it could remove or overlap neighbours), so the mod does not force. `api.engine.util.proposal.makeProposalData` returns a proposal's `errorState` and `collisionInfo` without building it, so 1.1 checks a rebuild first; it takes a `SimpleProposal` (see "What a mod can and cannot change"). Asked about the same rebuild as a `SimpleProposal`, it agreed with the game on the buildings tested in "333": both refused buildings came back `Collision` (88894 with entities 80088 and 110793, 217856 with 126954), and on a building the game rebuilds, it came back clear for each of 19 buys, all of which landed.
- **Unknown.** What the accumulated costs are made of and whether they are charged as the building's upkeep or as vehicle maintenance.

## Depot values in the base game

Source: each depot's update script in `base/content/depots/{road,rail,water}.zip`, for example `rail/rail_depot.script.lua` and `road/road_maint_station.script.lua`. The values sit in the subconstruction's `depot` table (`maintenancePool`, `catchmentArea.radius`), its `metadata.emissions` (two states, idle and full), and the result's `cost` and `maintenanceCost`.

| Building | Pollution idle → full | Noise idle → full | Pool | Radius | Upkeep per year | Build cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rail depot | 100 → 120 | 50 → 100 | 6 | 800 | 100k | 6 × upkeep |
| Road depot | 80 → 100 | 40 → 80 | 12 | 400 | 30k | 6 × upkeep |
| Tram depot | 80 → 100 | 40 → 80 | 12 | 400 | 60k | 6 × upkeep |
| Water depot | 80 → 100 | 40 → 80 | 8 | 1000 | 100k | 6 × upkeep |
| Road maintenance | 40 → 80 | 20 → 40 | 16 | 600 (the script's capture params can override it) | 10k | 60k |
| Rail maintenance | 50 → 100 | 20 → 40 | 12 | 800 | 40k | 240k |
| Water maintenance | 50 → 100 | 20 → 40 | 12 | 1400 | 40k | 240k |

The rail depot's upkeep and cost are multiplied by its template's year coefficient (1, 3 or 9 by era, in `rail/rail_depot.con.lua`). For scale, processing industries reach 250 to 750 pollution and mines 10 to 30.

## How the game uses them at run time

- The `VEHICLE_DEPOT` component on the depot entity holds `maintenancePool`, `catchmentArea` and pool usage statistics (`api/tealdef/api/engine.d.tl`, record `VehicleDepot`). Ordinary depots and maintenance buildings both carry it; a maintenance building is a depot with no in or out nodes (`gui/entity_window/maintenance_station/maintenance_station.tl` in `gui.zip`).
- The depot entity is a subconstruction of the construction. The game finds the construction with `streetConnectorSystem.getConstructionEntityForSubconstruction` or `getConstructionEntityForDepot`, and the `CONSTRUCTION` component lists its `depots` (`api/tealdef/api/engine/system.d.tl`, `engine.d.tl`).
- The maintenance window shows pool usage as the count from `transportVehicleSystem.getVehiclesForMaintenanceStation(depot)` against `maintenancePool` (`gui/entity_window/entity_window_util.tl`). Each vehicle's `TRANSPORT_VEHICLE` component also names its `maintenanceStation`.
- Emissions: `game_mechanics/emission/emissions.script.tl` sets every emitter every few updates. For a depot, the emission state slides between the two `metadata.emissions` entries with vehicles in maintenance against the pool. The eco factor is applied only to industries; depots, stations and warehouses always get 1.
- Upkeep: the construction entity's `MAINTENANCE_COST` component holds the script's `maintenanceCost`. `api.engine.util.maintenance.calcMaintenanceForSubconstruction(depot)` returns the figure actually charged, 1.25 times that in the test save (the base config's `infrastructureMaintenanceScale`).
- Maintenance assignment, seen in game: a vehicle's `maintenanceStation` names no existing station while it has none (probably the empty id). The game fills it within about a day, and the link then mostly stays; some vehicles lose it now and then in normal play and are assigned again, sometimes to another building.

## What a mod can and cannot change

- `api/tealdef/api/cmd.d.tl` has no command that changes a depot's pool or radius. The only maintenance command there sets a maintenance cost, and it is marked debug (`makeMaintenanceCostUpdateCmd`). `makeEntitySetEmissionsCmd` sets an emitter's noise power, pollution power and radius and is not marked debug; `makeComponentExchangeCmd` swaps an `EmissionEmitter` or `ModelInstanceList` component and is marked debug. Values set this way would last only until the building's next rebuild (a module edit, catenary), which reruns its update script, so the mod does not use them; pool and radius need a rebuild in any case.
- Checking a proposal: `api.engine.util.proposal.makeProposalData` takes a `SimpleProposal`. Its type definition says `Proposal` (`api/tealdef/api/engine/util.d.tl`), but handed the `Proposal` from `createProposalReplaceConstruction` the game raises `bad argument #2 to 'makeProposalData' (SimpleProposal expected, got Proposal)`. A replacement as a `SimpleProposal` removes the construction and adds a `ConstructionEntity` with the same `fileName` and `transf` and the new params, with `old2new = { [entity] = 0 }`, as the base landmarks do (`landmarks/landmarks.script.tl`; also `mission/mission_framework/mission_framework_util_entity.tl`). Both send theirs with `ignoreErrors` on; the base building windows send theirs with it off (`gui/entity_window/bridge_and_tunnel.tl`, which reads the errors through `builtin.ProposalViewer`'s `onCreateProposalData`).
- `api.engine.util.proposal.createProposalReplaceConstruction(entity, params)` builds a proposal that replaces a construction with new parameters (`api/tealdef/api/engine/util.d.tl`). A game script sends it with `api.cmd.makeWorldBuildProposalCmd(proposal, nil, false, false, false)` from `update`, without a callback.
- A mod's `runFn` can register `addModifier("loadConstruction", fn)` and change any construction definition as it loads. The base game does this itself (`base/base_mod.lua` in `base.zip`).
- Construction params are 1-based: the catenary param's `defaultIndex = 2` means "Yes", and `rail/rail_depot.script.lua` tests `params.catenary == 2` (`scripts/construction/param_util.tl` in `scripts.zip`). A param can be declared with `postConstructionModifiable = false` (`api/tealdef/api/type.d.tl`, record `ConstructionScriptParam`).
- Depot `.con` files name their update script relative to their own folder (`"rail_depot.script@updateFn"`). `scripts/util.tl` `useFn` loads a script function from an absolute `file@function` reference.
- A construction script can be Teal: the base heliport's module update script is a `.script.tl` file that returns a table (`stations/air/heliport/modules.script.tl`).
- Telling a depot construction apart: every base depot and maintenance `.con` names `configureDepotHudIconsFn` with a `depotType` in its templates. The Roundhouse mod's depot does not, but its files sit under `depots/`. The Boathouse mod's maintenance building (`assets/ingo_bootsschuppen/ingo_bootsschuppen_maint.con.lua`) has neither marker, but declares `constructionType = "WATER_DEPOT"`, as the Roundhouse and Modern Railway Depot mods declare `"RAIL_DEPOT"`; the engine's `ConstructionType` has three depot values, `STREET_DEPOT`, `RAIL_DEPOT` and `WATER_DEPOT` (`api/tealdef/api/type.d.tl`). The base depot `.con` files do not set it. The base path signals (`infrastructure/signal/signal_path_a.con.lua`, `signal_path_c.con.lua`) name the same HUD script; they are edge objects (`edgeObject`), which the depots are not.
- The update script also sets the building's upkeep (`result.maintenanceCost`), so the wrapper can scale it in the same rebuild.
- Modules: the game runs a module's update script once per built module, after the construction's update script (`ModuleDesc.updateScript` in `api/tealdef/api/type.d.tl`), with the arguments capture params, result, transform, tag, slot id, addModel and the construction's params (`stations/street/modular_street_station/modules.script.lua`). A module's upkeep and price are its `metadata.maintenanceCost` and `metadata.price`, which the game adds to the building's; no module script adds them. The construction's update script sees every built module's metadata in `params.modules` (`stations/street/modular_street_station/modular_terminal.script.lua` reads it). A mod can wrap them with `addModifier("loadModule", fn)`, which TF3 calls with the module's file name and definition, as TF2 did; the base game itself registers none (`base/base_mod.lua`).
- Charging money: a game script books a journal entry with `api.cmd.makeJournalBookAssetCmd(player, entry, position)`, the way the base marketing tool charges for a campaign (`gui/construction/tools/marketing_campaign_tool.script.tl`). The categories are in `scripts/journal.d.tl` (base tealdef): type `CONSTRUCTION` with subcategory `DEPOT`, type `MAINTENANCE` with `INFRASTRUCTURE`, and so on.
- No command sets a vehicle's maintenance station (`api/tealdef/api/cmd.d.tl`).

## The building window

- Clicking a depot or a maintenance building opens `MaintenanceStationWindowContent` for its `VEHICLE_DEPOT` entity (`gui/entity_window/make_entity_window.tl` in `gui.zip`). A construction with one such subconstruction opens it directly; one with several gets a tab per subconstruction.
- That window collects cards from `MaintenanceStationEowExtensionPoint` (`gui/entity_window/maintenance_station/maintenance_station_eow.script.tl`). The base cards are registered by resources of type `react-plugin ::MaintenanceStationEowExtensionPoint` with a `filePath`, an optional `condition` and an `order` (pool 20, balance 30, statistics 40, vehicles 50; for example `maintenance_station_balance.res.lua`). The card function receives the depot entity, the ownership state and the construction entity (nil while the construction cannot be modified).
- The other route is a construction's own `entityWindowScript`, as the Cleaning Plant uses (`game_mechanics/emission/pollution_cleanup_facility_hud.script.tl`): it replaces the whole window and adds custom tabs. Industry Upgrades uses the plugin route for industries.
- A GUI script sends an event to game scripts with `api.cmd.makeScriptingSendEventCmd("", id, name, param)`; a game script receives it in `handleEvent` once it has called `state:subscribeToEvent(name)`. The company window's rank claim works this way (`game_mechanics/company/company.tl` and `company_growth.script.tl`).
- The mod finds the building's depot as the first entry of the construction's `depots`, or, when that list is empty, the first subconstruction with a `VEHICLE_DEPOT` component, as the base window does.
- The game calls a construction's `entityWindowFn` many times while one window opens (about 60 in the first second), so the window script logs each building once.
- Widgets: `builtin.Button` takes `content` and `onClick`; any widget's `meta.enabled` greys it out (`scripts/builtin.d.tl`, `scripts/react.d.tl` in base tealdef).

## Company rank

- `company_progression_util.getCompanyProgressionState(player)` (`game_mechanics/company/company_progression_util.tl`) reads the state of `company_progression.gs`: `level` is the rank claimed in the company window, `potentialLevel` the rank earned. Rank rewards count from `level`.
- That util is a thin reader: it takes the game script's state from `gameScriptSystem.getEntityForGameScript("::game_mechanics/company/company_progression.gs")` (its `GAME_SCRIPT` component, `companyState[player]`), and `company_growth_config.res` can override both ranks with an `enforcedLevel`. The mod reads through the util and falls back to the same state, so the rank gate still works if the util file moves.

## Pollution tools in the base game

- The Industry Greenification tool (`gui/construction/tools/industry_greenify_tool.script.tl`, permit `ECO_INDUSTRY` at ranks 3, 6, 9, 12 and 15) cuts an industry's emissions by 80% (`emissionReductionPerEcoLevel` 0.8). Its handler stores any entity, but the emissions script only applies the eco factor to industries.
- The Cleaning Plant (rank 10, uses machines) removes 10,000 pollution at full output.
- A town's pollution rating averages samples over the town's whole area, so every depot inside the town border counts.

## How the map shows pollution

- The pollution layer (`gui/layers/layer_pollution.tl`) colours the ground from the emission grid's averaged value (`visualizationType` `DecibelAverage`) in `pollutionNumSteps` 12 steps between `pollutionLowerBoundDB` 57 and `pollutionUpperBoundDB` 80 (`game_mechanics/emission/emission_config.tl`). The noise layer does the same with 12 steps from 63 to 85 dB.
- The number beside the cursor (`gui/main/game_tooltips.tl`) reads `emissionGridSystem.getInterpolatedEmission(position, true, type)`, the averaged value, converts it with `api.util.powerToDecibel` and maps it to the steps: a reading of n (1 to 11) is 57 + 1.92 n to 57 + 1.92 (n + 1) dB, 12 is 80 dB or more, 0 is below about 59 dB. `getInterpolatedEmission(position, false, type)` gives the live value.
- So a cut in a building's pollution power moves the map by 10 log10 of the factor: level 1 (×0.95) −0.2 dB, level 5 (×0.75) −1.2 dB, level 9 (×0.55) −2.6 dB, level 15 (×0.25) −6.0 dB; at 1.92 dB a step that is 0.1, 0.7, 1.4 and 3.1 steps. That is the most it can move, beside a building that is the only source. Noise is on the same kind of scale.
- The averaged grid follows a change slowly: the inner rings grow and shrink over many seconds as trains come and go. Measured at a building that is the only source, the averaged power closes the gap to the live power like an exponential with a time constant of about 190,000 game-clock units (fits of 172,000 to 195,000; at 4x speed about 48 real seconds). The live value is not instant either: right after a rebuild the emitter reads 0 until the emissions script sets it again a few updates later, and the live value dipped by only 0.8 dB in that time.
- Live value at the building, alone in a field: 10 log10(power) + 63.13 dB for pollution (power 50: 80.12 dB, 40: 79.15, 27.5: 77.52), within 0.01 dB, with the wind at 2.27 m/s throughout.
- Pollution has wind; noise does not. The pollution grid holds one global wind vector (`EmissionGrid.wind`, `wind : Vec2f`, "global wind direction", in `api/tealdef/api/engine.d.tl`), and the pollution layer draws it as an arrow with the speed in m/s in the lower right corner (`windRenderableConfig` in `gui/layers/layer_pollution.tl`). The pollution field is an oval blown downwind of the building, the noise field a circle around it. So what the cursor reads beside a building depends on the side it is on, and a change of wind could move a reading with no change in the building. A command sets the wind (`api.cmd.makeWorldChangeWindCmd(emissionGridEntity, wind)` in `api/tealdef/api/cmd.d.tl`); no base script calls it. The buy checks log the wind vector.
- A town's pollution rating (`town_util.getRatingPollution` in `game_mechanics/towns/town_util.tl`) sums the pollution over the town's area (at least 5 km²), converts the average to dB and maps 60 to 74 dB to a rating of 1 to 0. The town window splits a town's pollution by source, with "Depots" and "Maintenance Buildings" as their own rows (`town_util.getTownEmissionEmitters`, `game_mechanics/towns/town_react_util.tl`): a depot is a `VEHICLE_DEPOT` with in or out nodes, a maintenance building one without.
- A town's noise rating is local, unlike its pollution rating. `town_util.getRatingNoise` maps the engine's town noise in dB (`api.engine.util.getTownEmissionDB`) straight to the rating, with no division by the town's area. The town window's noise card splits it by district, residential and commercial, each with its capacity (`getTownNoisePerDistrict` in `game_mechanics/towns/town_react_util.tl`): the noise where people live and work. How the engine gathers it is not visible to scripts. So a noise level counts where the building stands next to houses and shops, and hardly at all in an empty field, while a pollution level counts the same anywhere inside the town's borders, spread over the whole area. Every point on the map belongs to some town's area (seen in game, not checked in the source), so moving a building out of one town makes its pollution another town's. The maintenance radius also ties a building to where its vehicles run.
- The source list's percentages are shares: `getTownEmissionEmitters` adds the emission of every emitter in the town's area (`getPollutionEmittersInSettlementArea`) by category and divides each category by the sum. An industry's emission state is 1 when idle, 2 when producing, plus 1 for each of two production boosts, and half that when its output storage is full (`game_mechanics/emission/emissions.script.tl`), so the industries' share moves in steps and drags every other row with it.
- The graph below the list is the town's `pollution` log, the rating as 0 to 1000 (`gui/entity_window/town/town_eow.script.tl`), with no live value added, unlike the Reputation graph. It took new points about every 30 seconds at 4x while the calendar was paused, each labelled with the paused year, so it follows game time.
- For scale: the Industry Greenification tool's 80% cut per eco level is −7 dB, about one full pollution tree.

## Other mods

- **Industry Upgrades** (Stardorn) gives industries levels with the same method: a `loadConstruction` modifier wraps every industry's update script and declares hidden level params; buying a level rebuilds the construction from the game script. It has no licence, so this repo reuses the method only, never its code. It wraps only constructions marked as industries, so a depot wrapper does not overlap it.
- **Modern Railway Depot** (MaikC, `maikc_depot_modern`) is modular too and declares `constructionType = "RAIL_DEPOT"`; its 12 modules sit under `depots/rail/depot_modern/` and are wrapped. It is available from 2015, or from 1900 with its mod setting `availability_start` ("Available from 1900", off by default).
- **Roundhouse** (a modular rail depot) builds one subconstruction with a `depot` table (pool 1, radius 800) and $60,000 upkeep, and adds its modules into it. Each stall and each "segment without a stall" adds 1 to the pool in its module script and $100 upkeep through its metadata; the chimney adds $50 and no vehicles. Its sliders allow 0 to 14 stalls on each side, so the pool can reach about 29.

## Unknowns

- How the game picks the building a vehicle is assigned to.
- What a rebuild sent with `ignoreErrors` on does when it collides: whether it overlaps or removes the colliding entities. A one-off probe on a copy of the "333" save (one forced rebuild of Berkeley Road Depot, 88894) would show it.
- How much of the pollution beside a busy depot comes from its vehicles rather than the building.
- Whether, when and how the wind changes in play, and how its speed changes the pollution beside a building (stronger wind probably carries more of it away). The wind is likely to change only over a long time, if at all: it read 2.27 m/s in every shot of one session, with no evidence yet either way.
- Whether the emission grid's average follows game time or the calendar. Most likely game time, as nearly every other mechanic does, and the averaging fitted game-clock units on a save whose calendar is slowed (the date stayed 12 January 1915); it has not been checked by comparing two calendar speeds.
